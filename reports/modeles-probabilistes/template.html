A final canopy cover model that says how sure it is

The final model draws three 10 m maps from Sentinel-2: a conservative map, a central map and an optimistic map. The true cover falls between the conservative and optimistic maps in 8 cells out of 10. Its central map is as accurate as the current model, and its error is about 40% lower than the best existing canopy cover map for Ontario.

Branch tsalin/reproduction-and-verification · code and full protocol in modeles-probabilistes/ · protocol and selection rule set on November 17, 2026, before training · 85 trainings, 5-fold cross-validation by site, external test on 15 new Ontario lidar sites.

In brief

  • Final model: the project's U-Net (ResNet-34 encoder, ImageNet weights) with three outputs, the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles of cover, trained 5 times with different seeds and combined, then calibrated.
  • Architecture tests: the three outputs cost nothing in accuracy (central map MAE 0.0894 vs 0.0892 for the current single-output model). An encoder pre-trained on Sentinel-2 (ResNet-50, SSL4EO) did not beat ImageNet weights: better in 3 folds out of 5, where 4 were required.
  • Calibration: one correction per band of predicted cover brings the 80% interval to 79% coverage in partly forested cells, instead of 68% with a single correction for the whole map.
  • Against existing maps: on 15 new Ontario sites, the central map's MAE is 0.087, against 0.155 for SCANFI and 0.164 for Meta/WRI, even after recalibrating both on the project's lidar. The final model has the lowest error on all 15 sites.

What the three maps mean

For each 10 m pixel, the model gives a range of plausible canopy cover, as a fraction from 0 to 1.

  • Conservative map (q10). The true cover is below it in only 10% of cells. Use it when overestimating cover is costly.
  • Central map (q50). The median, the best single estimate. It replaces the current model's map.
  • Optimistic map (q90). The true cover is above it in only 10% of cells.

The gap between the conservative and optimistic maps is the uncertainty. It is narrow over water and closed forest, and wide over partial cover, clearcuts and regrowth.

How the model learns three maps. The network has three outputs instead of one. Each is trained with a quantile (pinball) loss that penalizes errors on one side more than the other: for q10, overestimates cost 9 times more than underestimates. At 50%, this loss is half the L1 loss of the current model, so the central map is still the median.

Truth here means the project's lidar label: the share of the 10 m cell under canopy above 2 m, from leaf-on airborne lidar.

How the candidates were compared

The project's test set has 3 sites, and its MAE ranges from 0.078 to 0.134 between them. That spread is larger than the expected difference between two architectures, so every candidate was evaluated on all 49 sites by cross-validation.

Cross-validation by longitude. The 49 project sites are sorted from west to east and cut into 5 blocks of 9 or 10 sites. Each block is predicted by models trained on the other 4, so every prediction is for a region the model has not seen.

External test. The 15 new Ontario sites from the earlier verification study are never used for training or for any choice. The 7 NEON sites in the United States are reported for description only.

Selection rule, set before training. The encoder pre-trained on Sentinel-2 replaces the ImageNet one only if its quantile score is better in at least 4 folds out of 5.

Architecture tests

All figures in this section are out-of-fold: each site is predicted by models that never saw it. Pooled over the 49 sites, that is 11.9 million cells of 10 m.

Three outputs instead of one

The quantile model's central map has the same MAE as the current model (0.0894 vs 0.0892) and the same R² (0.850). The extra outputs come for free.

Five seeds instead of one

Combining 5 trainings lowers the MAE from 0.0907 to 0.0894 and the quantile score from 0.0291 to 0.0285. Each seed is treated as a distribution, and the final percentiles are those of the mixture of the 5, so the interval widens where the seeds disagree.

Encoder pre-trained on Sentinel-2

The ResNet-50 pre-trained on SSL4EO-S12 is better in folds 2, 3 and 5, and worse in folds 1 and 4. The rule required 4 folds out of 5, so the lighter ResNet-34 stays. Pooled, it is also slightly worse (MAE 0.0908 vs 0.0894), and the external test confirms it: 0.0946 vs 0.0875 on the 15 new sites.

This comparison mixes two changes, pre-training and depth: the published SSL4EO weights do not exist for ResNet-34.

Calibration

Before calibration, the interval holds 69% of labels instead of 80%. Conformal calibration fixes this by shifting each bound by a constant measured on held-out data: the 90th percentile of how far labels fall below the conservative map, and the same above the optimistic map.

A single pair of constants reached 80% overall, but only as an average. Cells predicted near 0 or near 1 (water, closed forest) were covered 89% of the time, while partly forested cells were covered 68% of the time.

The final model therefore uses one pair of constants per band of 0.1 of the central map. In cross-validation, each fold's constants come from the other 4 folds, so the coverage below is honest. The final constants come from all 49 sites. This per-band step was added after the first evaluation showed the imbalance; it was not in the protocol set before training.

Final model vs existing canopy cover maps

Three national or global products, put on each site's 10 m label grid with the project's own code. Every comparison uses only cells where the label and all maps are valid.

  • Meta/WRI Global Canopy Height v2 (2026): about 1 m canopy height from very high resolution imagery; cover is the share of 1 m pixels above 2 m, the same definition as our label.
  • SCANFI v2 (2026): crown closure at 30 m from Landsat, calibrated on the National Forest Inventory. The 5-yearly layer closest to the lidar year.
  • NTEMS 2022: canopy cover at 30 m from Landsat, calibrated on lidar plots.

Recalibration. These products define cover differently. As in the project, each is also shown after a monotone (isotonic) mapping to our lidar label, learned without the evaluated sites: on the other 4 folds in cross-validation, and on the 49 project sites for the new sites. This compares models rather than definitions.

Our models. In cross-validation, the out-of-fold central map and the current model retrained in the same folds. At the new sites, the final models and the project's original 3-seed model.

Are the intervals honest away from the training sites?

The final models, with the constants learned on the 49 project sites, applied unchanged to the external sites.

Ontario. At the 15 new sites, 84% of labels fall inside the interval, and every site is between 75% and 90%. The rank correlation between a site's mean interval width and its MAE is 0.95: the map itself shows where it is less reliable.

United States. In forests similar to Ontario (NEON A), coverage holds at 83%. Out of domain (NEON B), it drops to 77% and the interval widens to 0.35 on average. At OSBS, a Florida pine savanna, the model mistakes green grass for canopy: MAE 0.208, coverage 74%.

What coverage means. It is a share of cells, pooled over sites. It does not promise 80% at every site or in every landscape.

Limitations and next steps

Limitations

  • The intervals describe disagreement with the lidar label, label noise included. They are calibrated on 49 managed Ontario forest sites.
  • Coverage is guaranteed on average over cells, not per site. At the new sites, partial cover is still slightly underestimated.
  • The existing maps are compared on our label definition. Our model learned that definition from the same lidar, which favors it.
  • The encoder test changes depth and pre-training at once, and uses the ResNet-34 learning rate for the ResNet-50.
  • Out of domain (low green vegetation, savanna), the central map overestimates cover and the interval holds fewer labels.

Next steps

  1. Publish the three maps with their meaning: the conservative map is the one to use for claims that must not overstate cover.
  2. Add a prediction script for new areas with the 5 final models and the per-band constants (today, maps are made for the evaluation sites only).
  3. Add northern and low-vegetation sites to training. This targets both the remaining bias at the new sites and the out-of-domain failures.
