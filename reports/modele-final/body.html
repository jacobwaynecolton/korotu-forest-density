The final canopy cover model, trained on 250 lidar sites

The final pipeline draws 250 Ontario lidar sites from a bank of 14,015 blocks, with extra weight on partial canopy cover, and trains a U-Net that gives three 10 m maps from Sentinel-2: conservative, central and optimistic. On 39 held-out sites it never saw, the central map's mean absolute error is 0.085, and the true cover falls between the conservative and optimistic maps in 79% of cells.

Branch tsalin/reproduction-and-verification · code, configuration and frozen site and scene lists in pipeline/ · all choices fixed before the run; the test set was used once, at the end.

In brief

  • Data: 250 sites of 5 × 5 km (64 from the earlier studies, 186 new), flown 2019 to 2025, with 807 Sentinel-2 scenes from the leaf-on season. Half come from the third of blocks with the most partial cover.
  • Accuracy: MAE 0.082 in cross-validation (211 sites) and 0.085 on the held-out test (39 sites). On the same test sites, the best existing map (SCANFI, recalibrated) reaches 0.158, and the first-version U-Net 0.098.
  • Uncertainty: the calibrated 10–90% interval holds 81% of labels in cross-validation and 79% on the test, for a target of 80%.
  • Sparse forest stays the hardest: on the test, MAE 0.111 where partial cover is high, 0.056 where it is low. The intervals widen accordingly.
  • To watch: four test sites flown in 2020, in two areas near 48.5° N (around 82° W and 94° W), overestimate cover by 0.08 to 0.18.

The pipeline

Ten steps on Slurm. Each one resumes where it stopped, and every rule lives in one configuration file.

  1. Bank of blocksEvery 5 × 5 km block with 25 CHM tiles, one UTM zone and a 2019+ flight; NTEMS gives each a partial-cover score and a stratum.
  2. Site drawThe 64 existing sites first, then a random draw per stratum (50 / 30 / 20%), 15 km apart; flight dates and at least one clean Sentinel-2 scene checked; scene list frozen.
  3. LabelsShare of 0.5 m CHM pixels above 2 m in each 10 m cell; explicit no-data rule; tiles deleted after use.
  4. ImagesThe frozen scenes, 12 bands plus the scene classification, from 1 June to 15 September, under 1% cloud.
  5. Test set and foldsBy 100 km groups, balanced on lidar partial cover, with a 25 km buffer.
  6. Step count8,000, 16,000 or 32,000 steps, tried on fold 0.
  7. ChoiceSmallest count within 1% of the best quantile score.
  8. Training5 folds × 5 seeds, plus 5 final models: U-Net ResNet-34 with q10, q50, q90.
  9. EvaluationSeed mixtures, conformal calibration per band of the central map, cross-validation and test metrics.
  10. MapsCalibrated conservative, central and optimistic maps of every site, from models that never saw it.

What changed from the first version

  • One site rule instead of three selection scripts, and five times more sites.
  • More partial cover, so that sparse forest is in training and in the test.
  • A spatial test set and folds instead of 3 fixed validation and 3 test sites.
  • Leaf-on images only: May and late-September scenes had more error in 11 of 12 cases.
  • A frozen scene list, so the dataset can be rebuilt exactly.
  • Cloud masking per pixel, and one simple rule for the reflectance offset.
  • Three maps instead of one, with calibrated uncertainty.
  • Prediction for any area with the final models.

Each change rests on a study in modeles-probabilistes/ or independant-verification/.

The dataset

250 sites of 5 × 5 km across the province's leaf-on lidar coverage.

How long to train

The first version's 8,000 steps were tuned for 43 sites. With about 160 training sites per fold, three step counts were tried on fold 0.

32,000 steps gave the best score (0.0266) and the lowest MAE (0.083). The rule, set before the run, keeps the smallest count within 1% of the best; only 32,000 qualified. All later trainings used it.

16,000 steps scored worse than 8,000. With one seed per count, part of that gap is noise between runs.

Results

Cross-validation: each site predicted by the 5 seeds of the fold that never saw it. Test: the 5 final models on 39 sites kept aside from the start.

Strata: thirds of the 250 sites by share of labelled cells with partial cover. Sparse forest has twice the error of closed forest, and the model widens its interval there (0.33 against 0.19 on the test).

Limits and next steps

Limits

  • All sites are in Ontario's leaf-on lidar coverage, which leaves out the south of the province.
  • The intervals hold on average over cells. On the test, the conservative map is slightly too high, and four sites miss widely.
  • About 3% of images carry wildfire smoke or haze, which the 1% cloud rule does not catch.
  • The label is the lidar's own definition of cover (canopy above 2 m), and the model learned it from the same source.

Next steps

  1. Look at the four overestimated test sites, and add low-vegetation landscapes (peatland, marsh, regenerating cuts) to training.
  2. Decide whether to exclude smoky scenes, with a simple rule on the darkest blue pixels.
  3. Use pipeline/predict_area.py to map new areas with the 5 final models.

Reproducing the run

From comps1: bash pipeline/run.sh submits the ten steps with their dependencies; --from N resumes. The site list, scene list, splits, step count and calibration constants of this run are in pipeline/manifests/, and every table on this page comes from pipeline/results/.

Run time: about 1 hour for the data steps (bank, selection, labels, images) and about 4 hours of training with up to 11 GPUs at once. Disk: 4.4 GB of outputs.
